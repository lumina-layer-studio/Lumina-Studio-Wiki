# Open a Lumina 3MF in Your Slicer: Objects, Colors, and AMS Slots

> Screenshots show Bambu Studio in Simplified Chinese; setting names below match the English interface.



When you open a Lumina Studio `.3mf` file in your slicer, the spool that actually prints each region depends on four linked settings: the slicer's project-filament list, each object's filament number, the physical spools loaded on the printer, and your AMS or external-spool slot mapping—not the preview color shown on the mesh alone.

This tutorial walks through opening a Lumina `.3mf` as a full project in Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer, verifying object and filament assignments, and inspecting the sliced display surface layer by layer before printing.

## Before you begin

Have the following ready:

- The complete `.3mf` file generated and downloaded from Lumina Studio.
- Your actual printer profile and installed nozzle diameter.
- Every physical filament spool required by the project.
- For AMS, ACE, MMU, or other multi-material units, a note of which slot holds each spool.

If you received the `.3mf` from someone else, check which Lumina version, material profiles, and generation settings they used. Standalone `.stl` exports only store raw mesh geometry and do not preserve multicolor object assignments.

:::note[Scope of this guide]
This guide focuses on opening `.3mf` projects, mapping filaments, and inspecting sliced toolpaths. Choose layer height, line width, flow, and print speed to match your printer, nozzle diameter, and filament.
:::

## Step 1: Open the 3MF as a complete project

Launch Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer and choose **Open Project** to load the `.3mf` file from Lumina Studio (or double-click the `.3mf` to open it in your default slicer).

If prompted to choose between opening as a project or importing geometry only, select **Open as project**. Importing geometry alone strips plate layout, object color assignments, filament slots, and process settings.

Once loaded, verify that you see:

- One or more build plates.
- Expandable models, objects, and sub-parts in the **Objects** panel.
- A numbered list of project filaments.
- The complete model positioned on the build plate.

If the file loads as a single merged mesh without separate color parts in the object list, close the window and reopen the original `.3mf` via **Open Project**.

## Step 2: Check the printer and nozzle

In the top-left **Printer** selector, confirm that both the printer profile and nozzle diameter match your physical setup.

If the slicer warns that the project is incompatible with your printer or nozzle, check why before overriding settings:

1. Whether the wrong printer or nozzle was selected in Lumina **Settings**.
2. Whether the target printer preset has not been added to your local slicer yet.
3. Whether the `.3mf` was originally exported for a different machine.

If the hardware setting in Lumina was wrong, return to Lumina Studio, select your actual slicer, printer, and nozzle, and export a fresh `.3mf` rather than rebuilding process parameters by hand. If you intentionally switch the project to another compatible printer, recheck the filament list, layer height, build volume, and sliced preview afterward.

## Step 3: Understand the four mapping layers

Keep these four concepts distinct before editing colors or slots:

| Layer | What it represents | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project filament | The filament slot number, display swatch, and material preset stored inside the `.3mf` | The slicer's **Filaments** list |
| Object assignment | Which project-filament number is assigned to a specific object or part | The filament column next to each part in **Objects** |
| Physical spool | The actual roll of filament you plan to print with (material, brand, and color) | The physical spool and its label |
| Physical slot | Which AMS slot, multi-material feeder, or external spool holder holds that roll | Printer / AMS status and your loading notes |

For example, a part marked **3** in the object list uses **Project Filament 3**—that spool does not have to sit physically in AMS Slot 3. When sending the print, you can map Project Filament 3 to whichever AMS slot holds that spool. Likewise, on-screen swatches are visual labels only; the printed color comes from the physical spool loaded in the mapped slot.

## Step 4: Check the project-filament list

Review the numbered entries in the slicer's **Filaments** list and confirm that each entry's material type and swatch match the material profiles chosen in Lumina Studio:

- Check the material type (such as PLA vs. PETG) alongside color; two spools that look similar on screen are not interchangeable if they use different base materials or optical profiles.
- Project-filament numbers do not need to follow your physical AMS slot order.
- If the slicer substituted a missing local preset automatically, confirm that the replacement preset's material type and nozzle/bed temperatures suit your spool.

Update any mismatched preset or swatch in the **Filaments** list first—this updates the project filament definition before you check part assignments and physical slots.

## Step 5: Check every object's filament assignment

Switch to the **Objects** tab and expand the plate, model, objects, and parts. Because one visual color may be split across multiple disconnected regions—and backing plates, outlines, hanging loops, or magnet cutouts form separate parts—these counts rarely match one-to-one:

- Perceived colors in the original artwork.
- Project filaments generated by Lumina.
- Individual objects and sub-parts in the slicer tree.
- Physical feeder slots available on the printer.

Click through the objects and sub-parts and check the number in the filament column. If a part points to the wrong project filament, click its filament badge and assign the intended entry.

Assign filaments according to which physical spool should print that region rather than relying on part names alone. Part names are static labels—for instance, a reused project may still list a part named `Base - White` even after its assigned filament is changed to black. After changing a part's filament, double-check the 3D highlight and object tree to make sure you modified only the intended part rather than the parent assembly.

## Step 6: Map project filaments to physical slots

Once object assignments are set, match each project filament to the spools loaded on your printer:

1. Material types match (for example, PLA to PLA).
2. Each spool is the exact color calibrated for this project.
3. Each spool is loaded in the slot you plan to map it to.
4. External spools are not mistakenly mapped to an automatic feeder slot.

You can map slots manually in the print dialog, or use your slicer's AMS **Sync** button when the printer is online and filament detection is up to date. Syncing pulls slot presets and colors from the printer—always review both **object → project filament** and **project filament → physical slot** afterward to confirm nothing shifted unexpectedly.

:::warning[Slot mapping]
Project-filament numbers do not need to match AMS slot numbers. Map each project filament to whichever slot holds the right spool in the print dialog instead of physically shuffling spools to match numbers.
:::

## Step 7: Slice the plate and inspect by filament

Click **Slice Plate** and switch to the **Preview** tab—always verify colors in **Preview** rather than stopping on **Prepare**.

Set the color scheme to **Filament** and confirm that:

- Every primary color region uses the expected filament.
- Backing plates, borders, and mounting features use the intended material.
- No filament was silently merged or reassigned to another slot.
- No floating-region, unsliceable, or out-of-bounds warnings appear.

If an on-screen swatch looks slightly off compared to the real spool, check whether it is merely an approximate RGB swatch or a wrong slot assignment. Display-swatch differences do not affect extrusion as long as the slot mapping is right.

## Step 8: Inspect the display surface layer by layer



Orient the camera to view the finished display face—flip to the **Bottom** view if the artwork prints face-down against the build plate, or stay on the **Top** view for top-surface prints.

<img
  src={require('./assets/04-check-sliced-display-surface.webp').default}
  alt="Turn to the display surface and inspect the sliced layers"
/>

Drag the vertical layer slider on the right to step through the print starting from the display face:

1. First-layer outlines and colors appear cleanly where expected.
2. Layer-to-layer color transitions occur at the intended heights.
3. Fine lettering, thin strokes, and small color islands remain intact after slicing.
4. The backing plate is solid with no unintended gaps or missing layers.
5. Prime towers, brims, or supports pull from an appropriate filament.

**Prepare** shows raw mesh geometry; **Preview** shows the actual extrusion toolpaths your nozzle will lay down. If fine text or thin lines vanish after slicing, those features are narrower than your current nozzle and line width can resolve—scale up the print dimensions, switch to a smaller nozzle, enable Arachne variable walls, or simplify fine details in the source image before re-exporting.

## Troubleshooting

### Settings and color groups disappeared when opening the 3MF

You likely chose **Import geometry only** when opening the file. Close the project and reopen the `.3mf` with **Open Project**. If the `.3mf` targets a different printer or nozzle, update **Settings** in Lumina Studio and export a fresh project.

### The slicer replaced a project filament with the wrong material

This happens when a material preset used in the `.3mf` is missing locally, or when syncing with an AMS overwrites the project filament list. Recheck all three links in order: project-filament preset, object assignment, and physical slot mapping.

### The object tree has many more parts than there are colors in the image

Objects represent 3D mesh regions rather than palette entries. Disconnected islands, backing plates, borders, loops, and magnet cutouts each appear as separate parts—verify their filament badges individually.

### The Preview swatch color looks slightly different from my physical spool

Slicer swatches are approximate screen colors. As long as each object points to the right project filament and that project filament maps to the right physical slot, the printer will extrude the intended spool.

### Small text or thin lines show up in Prepare but disappear in Preview

**Prepare** renders triangle meshes, whereas **Preview** renders extruded toolpaths constrained by nozzle diameter and minimum line width. Increase the overall print size, use a finer nozzle (such as `0.2 mm`), or simplify sub-nozzle details in the artwork.

## Next steps

Once the sliced preview and slot mapping match your setup, send the job to your printer. On a first run with new profiles, print a small test piece first and jot down the Lumina material profiles, project-filament order, and AMS slot mapping for easy reference.

If toolpaths and slot mappings are accurate but the physical print looks off-color, check your calibrated material profiles, spool batch consistency, first-layer squish, and viewing light temperature.
