# Hue Protection

**Hue Protection** reduces noticeable hue shifts during color matching. Standard nearest-color matching minimizes overall CIELAB color difference, which can sometimes map a pure blue to a slightly purple recipe or a red to an orange one when total distance is smaller. Enabling **Hue Protection** increases the penalty for hue deviation so matches stay closer to the original hue family.

This setting adjusts candidate ranking only—it does not add filaments, alter model geometry, or modify slicer settings.

## Enable Hue Protection

1. Open **Converter → Core Settings → Advanced Settings**.
2. Turn on **Hue Protection**.
3. Rebuild the preview to apply the change.

  alt="Hue Protection in Lumina Studio Advanced Settings"
/>

Enabling the switch reveals the dependent [Dark Chroma Gate](../dark-chroma-gate/dark-chroma-gate.mdx) slider underneath, which controls which low-chroma dark pixels enter the neutral-dark candidate branch. Its default value is **15**; **0** is a special setting that routes all dark pixels into the neutral branch rather than turning it off. Leave the gate at **15** on your first pass so you can evaluate **Hue Protection** on its own.

## What changes during matching

With **Hue Protection** off, Lumina Studio ranks candidates primarily by overall CIELAB color distance. With **Hue Protection** on, lightness and chroma still factor into the score, but hue angle differences carry a higher cost, favoring recipes in the same or an adjacent hue family.

Matches remain bounded by the recipes available in your active LUT or Material Archive—if the palette has no close match in the target hue family, Lumina Studio selects the closest available recipe.

## When to use it

Compare previews with **Hue Protection** enabled when:

- A recognizable red, blue, or green shifts into a neighboring hue family (such as blue turning violet or red turning orange);
- Your artwork relies on distinct adjacent hues where color direction matters more than subtle lightness steps;
- Your active LUT already contains a recipe in the right hue family that standard matching passed over.

Grayscale artwork, low-chroma images, and palettes lacking the target hue will show little or no difference.

## Relationship with Precise Color Matching

**Hue Protection** and [Precise Color Matching](../precise-color-matching/precise-color-matching.mdx) are mutually exclusive. Turning on **Precise Color Matching** disables the **Hue Protection** toggle so only one candidate-selection rule applies at a time.

<img
  src={require('./assets/02-precise-disables-hue.webp').default}
  alt="Hue Protection disabled while Precise Color Matching is enabled"
/>

To compare the two approaches fairly, test each one separately while keeping the source image, LUT, and all other color controls unchanged.

## Checking the result

Inspect both key subject colors and surrounding highlights, shadows, and gradients before and after enabling the toggle. When finite recipes force a trade-off, tighter hue accuracy can slightly shift local lightness. Once the preview looks right, generate a fresh 3MF file before slicing—previously exported 3MF files do not update automatically.
