# Why Do Lumina Preview, Slicer Preview, and the Physical Print Look Different?

Lumina Preview, your slicer preview, and the finished print represent three distinct stages of the workflow:

- **Lumina Preview**: how your chosen color source and modeling settings assign colors and build 3D geometry.
- **Slicer preview**: the actual extrusion toolpaths generated for your printer, nozzle, line width, and filament mapping.
- **Physical print**: the real-world piece shaped by physical filament, machine calibration, first-layer squish, build-plate texture, and ambient lighting.

While screen previews and physical plastic naturally differ in brightness and surface sheen, every major outline, color region, and per-layer filament assignment should match from stage to stage. If you want a refresher on what “Layer 1,” predicted color, and per-layer colors represent in Lumina, see [How to Read Lumina Layer Preview](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/layer-preview-guide/).

## Identify the type of difference first

| Symptom | Likely cause | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The slicer's color swatch looks off, but object and slot assignments are right | Display-only swatch color in the slicer | Safe to continue with final pre-print checks |
| An object, color region, or backing points to the wrong project filament or hardware slot | Incorrect filament mapping | Fix object and slot mapping, then slice again |
| Lumina and the slicer match, but the print looks greenish, gray, dark, or warm overall | Archive mismatch, filament batch, print calibration, or lighting | Run a small test swatch to isolate the variable |
| Small text, thin lines, or tiny color islands vanish after slicing | Print size, nozzle diameter, or line-width limits | Scale up the model, thicken fine strokes, or switch to a finer nozzle |
| Lumina Preview itself is black, green, perforated, or distorted | Source Type, input file, or modeling mode issue | Resolve the preview inside Lumina before exporting a 3MF |

Pay special attention to the first two rows: a slicer's colored square is merely an on-screen label and will not alter extrusion on its own, whereas assigning an object to the wrong project filament—or mapping that project filament to the wrong AMS slot—changes which spool actually prints.

## Seven-step diagnostic order

  src={require('./assets/difference-troubleshooting-flow.webp').default}
  alt="Seven-step diagnostic order from software version and color data to viewing light"
/>

Work through the checks below from upstream to downstream before adjusting nozzle temperature, flow, or source artwork.

### Step 1: Check Color Data Source and archives

Confirm whether **Color Data Source** is set to Material Archive or Classic LUT, and verify that:

- The selected archive or LUT file is intact and readable.
- Material type, brand, product line, and color match the physical spools you plan to print.
- You ran a fresh preview after changing color sources.
- For a Classic LUT, layer count, Layer Height, and filament order match your current project settings.

If Lumina Preview already looks wrong at this stage, address the color source first. See [Material Archives or Classic LUT?](../material-profile-selection/material-profile-selection.mdx).

### Step 2: Verify four-level filament mapping

Align all four links in the chain:

1. Archives or palette slots chosen in Lumina
2. Project filaments embedded in the exported 3MF
3. Project-filament numbers assigned to each object or part in the slicer
4. Physical spools loaded in your AMS, multi-material unit, or external holder

Synchronizing your AMS updates hardware slot labels in the slicer, while you still confirm that each project filament maps to the right physical spool. For step-by-step mapping instructions, see [Open a Lumina 3MF in Your Slicer](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).

### Step 3: Check nozzle, line width, Layer Height, and print size

Fine 3D geometry visible on the **Prepare** tab will only generate toolpaths if it is wide enough for your extruder line width. Verify that:

- Printer model and nozzle size in the slicer match your hardware.
- Line width can accommodate small lettering, thin outlines, and tiny isolated patches.
- Layer Height for the color layers matches your Lumina calibration source and project settings.
- Overall print dimensions have not been scaled down past your nozzle's resolution limit.

Layer Height governs Z-axis color stacking, whereas nozzle diameter and line width determine XY detail capacity. See [What Image Resolution Is a 0.2 mm or 0.4 mm Nozzle Equivalent To?](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/nozzle-equivalent-resolution/).

### Step 4: Inspect Lumina 2D and 3D previews

Review the 2D color-matched preview and the 3D geometry preview to confirm that:

- Outer silhouettes and transparent cutouts are clean.
- Key color regions have not merged or shifted unexpectedly.
- Backing, outlines, and optional hardware features match your settings.
- Eyes, lettering, and fine lines remain intact.
- There are no solid-black blocks, severe color casts, pinholes, or misaligned borders.

If the Lumina preview already shows missing or miscolored regions, adjust the source image, modeling mode, or palette before exporting to the slicer.

### Step 5: Slice the project and inspect the viewing face

Run **Slice** in Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer, or your preferred slicer and switch to the **Preview** tab (the **Prepare** tab shows raw meshes rather than actual toolpaths).

If the viewing face prints face-down on the build plate, orbit underneath the plate and scrub the layer slider inward from Layer 1:

- First-layer outlines and color regions are complete.
- Every layer calls the intended project filament.
- Fine strokes and tiny islands produce real extrusion paths.
- Backing, outlines, and keychain loops use the expected filament.
- No automatic filament substitutions, missing layers, floating parts, or bed-boundary warnings appear.

When Lumina Preview looks clean but sliced layers do not, check that the 3MF was opened as a full project and verify object-filament assignments, nozzle size, and line widths.

### Step 6: Inspect first layer, flow, build plate, and hardware

When both software previews look accurate yet fine details blur, peel off, break, or look blotchy on the print, check the physical setup:

- First-layer Z offset (too much squish causes color bleed; too little adhesion lets small islands lift).
- Build plate cleanliness, flatness, and surface compatibility.
- Nozzle temperature and flow ratio for the specific filament.
- Nozzle tip residue, partial clogs, or travel dragging across fresh lines.
- Filament moisture or noticeable batch-to-batch translucency differences.

Adjust one parameter at a time on a small test crop so you can tell exactly which change solved the issue.

### Step 7: Evaluate lighting and camera effects last

Monitors emit light directly, whereas printed plastic reflects and transmits surrounding light. Warm desk lamps, colored walls, and smartphone auto white balance, exposure, and HDR processing can introduce strong apparent color casts in photos.

Judge prints directly under steady, neutral lighting side by side. When photographing prints for comparison, turn off filters and auto enhancements and lock white balance.

## Five common scenarios

### Only the slicer's swatch color looks off

Click the object to check its assigned project-filament number and confirm which physical slot that filament maps to. Once the object assignment and physical spool match, editing the display swatch in the slicer is purely visual convenience.

### An object or slot is mapped to the wrong spool

Cancel the print, fix both the object-to-project-filament assignment and the project-filament-to-slot mapping, then re-slice and verify the layers (renaming an object label alone does not change which extruder prints it).

### Global color cast on the finished print

Double-check your archive selection and loaded spools first, then review batch consistency, filament dryness, print temperature, Layer Height, first-layer squish, and room lighting. If a spool consistently shifts away from an existing archive under controlled settings, print and extract a fresh gradient card for that batch.

### Tiny details blur, lift, or disappear

Check the slicer's layer preview to narrow down the cause:

- **No toolpath in Preview**: scale up the finished print, widen fine strokes in the artwork, or switch to a `0.2 mm` nozzle.
- **Toolpath exists, missing on print**: clean the build plate, tune first-layer squish and flow, and check for nozzle residue or wet filament.
- **Toolpath uses the neighboring color**: fix object and slot mapping.

### Lumina Preview turns black, green, or broken at the edges

1. Verify **Source Type**, your Material Archive or LUT, and **Base Plate Material**.
2. Re-run Preview after changing color sources.
3. Check the source image's alpha channel, color profile, and pixel dimensions.
4. For SVGs, inspect open paths, fill rules, `viewBox` bounds, and live effects—see [Troubleshooting SVG Uploads, White Backgrounds, Holes, and Missing Detail](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/svg-troubleshooting/).
5. Test with a simple flat PNG or minimal single-shape SVG to see whether the issue is file-specific.

## Helpful files to keep for support

If an issue persists, save the files from all three stages so it is clear where the discrepancy begins:

- Original input image or SVG
- Selected Source Type, archive or LUT name, and filament stack order
- Screenshots of Lumina's 2D and 3D previews
- Exported 3MF project file
- Slicer Preview screenshots of the viewing face and key color layers
- Printer model, nozzle size, Layer Height, line width, and physical slot mapping
- A neutral-light photo of the physical print

## Common questions

### Lumina Preview and slicer preview look different. Is it safe to print?

Only if the sole difference is an approximate display swatch color in the slicer while every color region, object assignment, physical slot, and sliced toolpath matches. If regions, filament mappings, or toolpaths differ, fix them and slice again before printing.

### Thin lines appear in Lumina Preview but vanish after slicing. Should I tweak colors?

Color adjustments rarely restore dropped geometry. Check whether a toolpath exists in the slicer's Preview tab; enlarging the print, thickening thin strokes, or switching to a smaller nozzle addresses line-width limits directly.

### Both previews look good, so why does the physical print look darker?

Backlit screens naturally appear brighter than reflective plastic. If the print is noticeably and consistently too dark, check that dark backing or shadow filaments were not swapped into upper slots, verify color-layer height and first-layer thickness, and inspect the print under bright neutral light.

### The print looks accurate in person, but greenish in phone photos. Should I recalibrate the archive?

No need. Smartphone auto white balance and indoor LED lighting frequently skew camera colors. Lock white balance under neutral daylight-balanced lighting with a white reference card in frame if you need accurate photos.

### Small lines look “wiped away” on the plate—is retraction to blame?

Check the sliced Preview first. If no path was generated, the feature fell below your line width. If the path is present in Preview but lifts during printing, clean the build plate and inspect first-layer adhesion, flow, and nozzle buildup before tuning retraction.
