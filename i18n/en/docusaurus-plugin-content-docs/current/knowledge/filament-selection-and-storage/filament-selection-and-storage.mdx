# How Should I Choose and Store Filament for Lumina 2.0?

When choosing filament for Lumina Studio, start with spools your printer handles reliably, whose exact product identity you know, and that match a verified Material Archive—before comparing approximate colors on screen.

A generic label like "red PLA" is not enough to predict layered color results. Material family, brand, product line, shade, batch, surface finish, light transmission, moisture level, and the selected Material Archive all shape the final print.

## Five steps for choosing or buying filament

### 1. Check printer, feed system, and nozzle compatibility

Review the specifications from both the filament and printer manufacturers first:

- Does the printer and automatic feed system support this material, spool size, and stiffness?
- Are the nozzle material and diameter rated for any fillers in the filament?
- Does your slicer include a suitable material preset for your printer?
- Do your build plate, temperature limits, enclosure, and ventilation meet the material's requirements?

Glow-in-the-dark, carbon-fiber, glass-fiber, metal-filled, wood-filled, and other heavily filled filaments can be abrasive and prone to clogging fine nozzles. Before loading them through a 0.2 mm nozzle, standard brass nozzle, or automatic material system, verify hardware compatibility and switch to a hardened or larger-diameter nozzle when required.

### 2. Choose the material family before the color

Both PLA and PETG work well for flat multicolor prints, but they differ in print temperature, first-layer adhesion, flow behavior, stringing, surface gloss, and interlayer bonding. Because PLA and PETG adhere poorly to each other—a property often used for breakaway support interfaces—stacking them directly in a color print can cause delamination.

Stick to a single material family within one artwork whenever possible. If you need to combine different materials in the same print, verify the following first:

- Your printer and slicer support the material combination.
- Each project filament keeps its own material preset.
- Adjacent layers bond reliably in your part geometry.
- Purge volumes and nozzle temperature transitions prevent color contamination.
- A small test swatch passes inspection before you start the full print.

### 3. Account for surface finish and light transmission

Spools that share a color label such as "white" or "red" can behave very differently when stacked and should not automatically share the same archive:

| Filament type | Impact on layered color results | What to check before printing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic solid color | Consistent baseline, though brands and batches still vary | Brand, product line, shade, and batch |
| Matte | Matte additives and surface scattering shift brightness and transmission | Validate separately rather than reusing a basic PLA archive of the same color |
| Silk or metallic | Viewing angle and lighting direction strongly affect perceived color | Evaluate test prints under consistent lighting |
| Clear, translucent, or natural | Backing and lower layers show through across more layers | Calibrate a custom archive and check both white- and black-backed cards |
| Glow, wood, metal, carbon fiber, and other fillers | Alters optics while increasing nozzle wear, clogging, or feed resistance | Confirm nozzle and feeder requirements before using fine nozzles or multi-material units |
| Recycled, blended, or batch-variable filament | Spools sold under the same name can shift noticeably | Print a test swatch for each new batch and re-verify for color-critical projects |

### 4. Match every physical spool to its Material Archive

Before generating a print, confirm these five details:

1. Material family
2. Brand and product line
3. Commercial color name, shade code, and finish
4. The Layer Height used when the archive was calibrated
5. The physical spool and feeder slot loaded on the printer

On-screen swatches and HEX codes help you identify filaments in the interface, but they cannot capture how light passes through stacked layers over different backing colors. To decide between color sources, see [Material Archives or Classic LUT?](../material-profile-selection/material-profile-selection.mdx).

### 5. Confirm with a physical test swatch

Before committing to a full-size piece, run a quick end-to-end check: **source image → Lumina Preview → slicer layer preview → physical test print**. Print the swatch with the exact spools, slicer presets, nozzle, color Layer Height, and backing setup planned for the final artwork.

If the printed swatch diverges from Preview, check spool identity, archive selection, slot mapping, and filament dryness first before editing the source image.

## How TD relates to a Lumina Material Archive

TD stands for **Transmission Distance**. In filament-painting workflows, it measures—in millimeters—the thickness of solid filament needed to block most transmitted light. Lower-TD filaments hide underlying colors in fewer layers, while higher-TD filaments let lower layers blend through a deeper stack.

In Lumina 2.0, a single TD number cannot replace a full Material Archive:

- TD alone does not capture hue shifts, brightness, or differences across red, green, and blue channels.
- It cannot stand in for measured color gradients over both white and black backings.
- It does not fully model matte additives, silk sheen, fillers, or surface light scattering.
- Different measurement setups, light sources, sample thicknesses, and batches often yield different TD readings.

Lumina 2.0 Material Archives are extracted directly from photographed gradient cards printed over white and black backings, capturing full optical fitting data for color prediction. An online TD value is a handy gauge of general translucency, while a matched Material Archive provides the multi-channel data needed for accurate previews.

## Storing and tracking individual spools

### Label each spool when you open it

Attach a label to the spool or its storage bag with:

- Material, brand, product line, color, shade code, and batch
- Date opened
- Matching Lumina Material Archive name
- Calibration Layer Height and last verification date
- Drying log, print notes, and approximate remaining weight

Keep leftover lengths with their original spool rather than combining similar shades in one bag, so every spool stays reliably linked to its calibrated archive.

### Sealed storage vs. active drying

Store idle spools in resealable bags or airtight dry boxes with fresh desiccant. Keep containers closed between prints and check gaskets and desiccant indicators periodically.

A passive dry box maintains low humidity and slows further moisture absorption, whereas damp filament requires active heated drying to drive moisture out of the polymer.

### Dry according to manufacturer specifications

Heat tolerance varies across filament blends and spool plastics—too little heat leaves moisture behind, while excessive heat can fuse strands or warp the spool:

1. Follow the drying temperature and duration specified by the filament manufacturer.
2. Verify that the spool plastic is rated for that temperature.
3. Use a dedicated filament dryer with steady temperature control.
4. Transfer the spool straight into sealed, low-humidity storage once drying finishes.
5. Log the drying date on the spool's label.

### Troubleshooting stringing methodically

Absorbed moisture often causes rough surfaces, micro-bubbles, popping sounds at the nozzle, blobs, weaker layer adhesion, or extra stringing. However, stringing can also stem from nozzle temperature, retraction settings, long travel moves, or the polymer's natural viscosity.

Test one variable at a time: print the same small calibration piece before and after active drying first, then fine-tune temperature or retraction if stringing persists.

## One-minute pre-print checklist

- Spool labels match the Material Archives selected in Lumina.
- Every project filament in the slicer uses the right material preset.
- Each physical spool is loaded into its assigned feeder slot.
- Filament is dry, unknotted, not brittle, and feeding smoothly.
- Filled filaments meet nozzle material, diameter, and feeder requirements.
- You ran Preview and sliced again after any change to archives, Layer Height, or spools.
- A small test swatch passed before starting a large print.

For step-by-step slot assignment in your slicer, continue with [Open a Lumina 3MF in Your Slicer](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).

## Common questions

### Can I calibrate archives for both PLA and PETG?

Yes. As long as your printer, slicer, and workflow support the material, you can create and use dedicated Material Archives for either PLA or PETG. Because print temperatures and optical behavior differ between polymers, keep separate archives for each material family, and test interlayer adhesion and purge temperatures before mixing materials in one print.

### If I don't have an exact color match, should I shop by the closest HEX code?

There is no need to hunt for an identical HEX code. Two filaments with similar on-screen swatches can transmit light very differently when stacked. Choose a dependable, clearly identified spool backed by a solid Material Archive, then evaluate the palette in Lumina Preview and with a quick test swatch.

### Do I need to recalibrate every new batch of the same filament?

Start with a small test swatch using your existing archive. If color, translucency, and surface finish match expectations, you can keep using it; if the new batch shifts noticeably and your project demands tight color consistency, run a re-verification or create a fresh archive.

### Can matte PLA share an archive with basic PLA of the same color?

Validate it separately first. Matte additives alter surface reflection and internal light scattering, which often shifts stacked brightness and hue compared to standard PLA. Create a dedicated archive whenever color accuracy matters.

### Is a higher TD always better for translucent filaments?

Neither high nor low TD is inherently better—they serve different roles. High-transmission filaments create gradual multi-layer color transitions, while lower-transmission filaments provide stronger coverage and crisp highlights. Pick filaments that suit your artwork, Layer Height, and palette, then confirm with Preview and a swatch.

### Can a 0.2 mm nozzle print wood-, carbon-fiber-, or glow-filled filaments?

Check both the filament and printer manufacturer specifications before trying. Fiber- and particle-filled materials frequently clog 0.2 mm nozzles, and abrasive additives like carbon fiber or glow powder typically require a hardened steel nozzle of 0.4 mm or larger. Only run filled filaments when both manufacturers explicitly support your nozzle and feeder setup.

### Why does my filament still string even though it's stored with desiccant?

Desiccant inside a sealed box slows new moisture absorption, but it rarely pulls existing moisture out of damp filament. Dry the spool actively per the manufacturer's instructions and retest; if stringing continues, inspect nozzle temperature, retraction settings, and travel paths.
