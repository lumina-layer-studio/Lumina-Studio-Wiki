# Material Archives or Classic LUTs?

Pick the color data source that matches your physical spools, target Layer Height, and project workflow:

- Start with an **installed Material Archive** when a verified profile already matches your exact filament brand, product line, and shade.
- Create a **custom Material Archive** when your spool isn't in the library yet or when your artwork demands tight color accuracy.
- Choose a **Classic LUT** when reopening a Lumina 1.x project or reusing a fixed multi-spool palette you have already validated.

In Lumina 2.0, **Color Data Source** offers **Material Archive** and **Classic LUT** (previously called Legacy LUT; the [Inferred LUT](../../reference/converter-basic/inferred-lut-source/inferred-lut-source.mdx) option is currently hidden). Terms like "installed" and "custom" simply describe how a Material Archive was created.

## How the two color sources compare

| Source Type | Best suited for | Key selection criteria | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material Archive | Mixing and matching individual single-spool profiles freely per artwork | Exact polymer family, brand, product line, shade, and calibrated Layer Height | If an installed archive doesn't match your physical spool, treat Preview colors as a rough estimate |
| Classic LUT | Reproducing a fixed, pre-validated color set or opening 1.x LUT files | Original Color Mode, channel filament order, layer count, and Layer Height | Swapping any physical spool in the set invalidates the original color mapping until retested |

Each conversion uses one active Source Type at a time. Whenever you switch sources, click **Preview** again to compute fresh recipes for the new palette.

## Material Archive: flexible palettes for Lumina 2.0

Each Material Archive captures how a single spool transmits and reflects light across printed gradient cards over white and black backings. During conversion, you pick whichever single-spool archives you want from the Material Library, then set **Color Layers**, **Layer Height**, and **Base Plate Material** for your artwork.

Archives in your local Material Library typically come from three places:

- Profiles downloaded on demand from the public cloud catalog or selected from an offline pack;
- Community-shared ZIP archives imported after checking their source details
- Custom archives you calibrated yourself from printed gradient cards

All three work identically in the converter picker: installed profiles get you started right away, while custom profiles reflect your exact spool, printer, and lighting conditions.

A new local library can be empty. Browse the catalog, download the profiles you need, then return to the picker to select them; there is no need to install the full library first. See the [Cloud Material Library](../../reference/materials-and-recipes/cloud-material-library/cloud-material-library.mdx) for product search, regional product names and offline packs.

### When an installed archive is a good starting point

Test an installed archive on a small swatch first when all of the following line up:

- Polymer family matches (PLA with PLA, PETG with PETG).
- Brand, product line (such as Basic, Matte, or Silk), and color name match.
- The archive loads cleanly with valid verification metrics.
- Your planned Layer Height matches the archive's calibrated Layer Height.
- You know the exact spool loaded on the machine rather than guessing from a similar shade.

Because pigment batches, moisture levels, and nozzle temperatures still introduce subtle shifts between spools sold under the same name, a quick physical test swatch is the fastest way to confirm accuracy before a large print.

### When to calibrate your own Material Archive

Create a custom archive whenever:

- Your filament brand, product line, polymer, or shade isn't in the Material Library.
- A test swatch using a similarly named archive shifts noticeably from Preview.
- Your filament uses translucent resins, silk sheen, matte additives, or specialty fillers, or varies visibly between batches.
- Your artwork relies on accurate portraits, smooth neutral grays, or exact brand colors.
- You want a dependable, traceable library tied directly to the labeled spools on your shelf.

To calibrate a new spool, follow [Generate and Print Gradient Calibration Cards](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/gradient-card-generate-print/) and [Photograph and Extract Gradient Cards](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/gradient-card-extract-profile/).

## Classic LUT: fixed palettes and 1.x projects

A Classic LUT stores a precomputed lookup table mapping target colors directly to multi-layer filament stacks. It remains handy for reproducing validated fixed palettes and working with LUT files saved from Lumina 1.x.

When printing with a Classic LUT, keep these four details aligned:

- The LUT's original **Color Mode** and channel order
- The exact physical filament assigned to each channel
- Supported layer count and **Layer Height**
- The nozzle and slicer settings used when that LUT was validated

Some Classic LUTs allow dynamic layer counts and heights, while others lock to a fixed configuration; if the interface indicates dynamic settings aren't supported for a given LUT, stick with its built-in layer parameters.

The Classic LUT uploader accepts `.npy`, `.json`, `.npz`, and `.msgpack` files. Keep in mind that a valid file load only confirms readable syntax—accurate print colors still depend on loading the exact spools the LUT was built for.

### 1. Material family

PLA, PETG, and other polymers differ in print temperature, crystallinity, light transmission, and surface gloss. Even if two spools look identical on the reel, keep separate archives for each material family.

### 2. Brand, product line, and shade

Generic labels like "red" or "white" span wide optical ranges. Match the exact brand, product line (for example, Basic PLA vs. Matte PLA), and shade code—and check which exact product a contributor measured before relying on a shared archive.

### 3. Layer Height and Color Layers

Every Material Archive logs the Layer Height used during calibration, while the converter lets you configure **Color Layers** and **Layer Height** for the artwork. Because changing layer thickness alters how much light each layer absorbs and transmits, keep your archive, converter settings, and slicer Layer Height aligned.

### 4. Physical spools and feeder slots

Lumina archive names, slicer project-filament slots, and physical feeder bays on your printer are three distinct links in the chain. After exporting your 3MF, map each project filament to its matching physical spool in the slicer rather than trusting approximate screen swatches—see [Open a Lumina 3MF in Your Slicer](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).

## Signs of a mismatched archive or LUT

Using an archive or LUT that doesn't match your loaded spools typically shows up as:

- Washed-out grays, unexpected color casts, crushed shadows, or clipped highlights right inside Lumina Preview
- A clean on-screen Preview that prints noticeably warmer, cooler, darker, or flatter on the build plate
- Unexpected filament combinations assigned to neutral or skin-tone regions
- Slicer material presets or feeder slots pointing to the wrong physical spool
- Repeat prints shifting in color after swapping to a different brand or fresh spool batch

Instead of editing your source artwork to compensate for a color shift, verify Source Type, archive identity, Layer Height, and slicer slot mapping first, then recalibrate if needed. For a step-by-step diagnostic order, read [Why Do Lumina Preview, Slicer Preview, and the Physical Print Look Different?](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/preview-slicer-print-differences/).

## Recovering missing or removed archives

1. Click **Refresh** in the converter picker or Material Library to rule out an unrefreshed list.
2. Open **Material Management → Material Library** to check whether the archive is still installed and intact.
3. If you saved an offline backup ZIP earlier, reinstall it via **Import Material ZIP**—see the [Material Archive management guide](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/material-library-management/).
4. If an older archive's origin is unknown, calibrate a fresh archive directly from your physical spool.
5. For repeatable production jobs, archive the source image, Lumina Share Card or settings, Material Archive ZIP (or LUT file), exported 3MF, and sliced project together in one folder rather than saving only a screenshot of profile names.

## Common questions

### An installed archive already matches my filament's brand and color—should I still calibrate my own?

Run a small test swatch with the installed archive first and compare Preview, sliced paths, and the physical print. If the colors match well, you're set; if your spool comes from a different sub-line (such as Matte vs. Basic) or shifts noticeably on the bed, calibrate a custom archive for your spool.

### If no archive matches my spool, can I pick the closest swatch color?

Using a rough color stand-in is fine while learning the interface, but avoid relying on it for finished prints. White, black, translucent, and filled filaments with similar surface swatches can have dramatically different transmission depths when stacked—print a test swatch or calibrate the spool first.

### Can one Material Archive cover both PLA and PETG?

No. Each archive models the measured optics of one specific filament formula; switching polymers changes print temperature, translucency, and surface finish. Maintain separate archives and slicer presets for each material family.

### Can I convert an older Classic LUT directly into 2.0 Material Archives?

No—a Classic LUT stores pre-blended multi-spool combinations, whereas 2.0 Material Archives store single-spool optical models, so renaming file extensions will not convert one into the other. Keep using **Classic LUT** mode for legacy projects, and print single-spool gradient cards when moving spools into the Material Archive workflow.

### Why did my Preview clear when I switched Source Type?

Switching between Material Archives and Classic LUTs changes the active palette, channel order, and optical recipe engine. Click **Preview** again to generate an accurate preview for the newly selected source.
