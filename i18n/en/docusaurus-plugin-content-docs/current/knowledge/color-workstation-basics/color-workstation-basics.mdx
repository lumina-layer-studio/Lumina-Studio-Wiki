# Color Workstation Basics: Select, Replace, Undo, and Free Color

Once an image has been previewed, **Color Workstation** lets you refine matched colors across the entire piece or within specific regions, apply custom recipes, or isolate selected shades as **Free Color** objects.

A typical editing pass follows six steps:

1. Run **Preview** to generate the initial color match and **Palette**;
2. Choose a selection tool that fits the scope of your edit;
3. Select the source color or region on the preview canvas or **Palette**;
4. Pick a reachable target color from your current Classic LUT or Material Archives;
5. Review the pending source-to-target pair and confirm the replacement;
6. Check the updated 2D, 3D, and **Layer Preview** views, then click **Generate** to export a fresh 3MF.

**Undo**, **Clear Remaps**, and **Clear Free Colors** act independently: **Undo** steps back one action, **Clear Remaps** resets all color replacements, and **Clear Free Colors** removes Free Color object markers without touching color edits.

## Availability and Session Behavior

**Color Workstation** operates on the active preview's matched color map and **Palette**, so it unlocks as soon as your first **Preview** completes.

Uploading a new image, switching Material Archives or Classic LUTs, or changing **Color Layers** or **Layer Height** rebuilds the underlying match—make fresh selections after any of those changes. Likewise, any 3MF exported before your color edits reflects the earlier state, so click **Generate** again when you finish editing.

## Choosing a Selection Tool

/>

### Same color

Selects every region across the entire image that shares the chosen matched color. You can also select multiple matched colors and merge them into a single target shade.

- **Best for**: Replacing a background shade everywhere, consolidating stepped color bands, or applying one **Custom Recipe** to an entire color group.
- **Keep in mind**: If a character's eyes and clothing share the same matched color, **Same color** selects both. Use **Pick blocks** when you want to edit an isolated feature.

### Similar colors

Click a region on the preview to select blocks within a color tolerance. Raising the tolerance widens the selection; setting tolerance to `0` selects only exact matches. Check the highlight overlay carefully near smooth gradients before confirming.

### Pick blocks

Click individual connected regions on the preview canvas to toggle them in or out of your selection—ideal for isolated details such as eyes, specular highlights, or lettering.

### Selection brush

Drag across the preview canvas and apply the stroke to select every contiguous color block touched by the brush. Adjust the brush size as needed and inspect the highlighted boundary before choosing a target color.

### Combining Tools

All four tools work on the same active selection, so switching tools keeps your current selection intact. Click **Select none** whenever you want to clear the selection and start fresh.

## Making a Color Replacement

### 1. Check the Base Preview First

Verify that the model's outer silhouette, transparency, and overall palette look sound before fine-tuning individual regions. Issues such as missing alpha boundaries, holes, or mismatched filament sets are best resolved in your source image or converter settings first.

### 2. Select the Target Scope

Pick a global or local selection tool and rely on the highlight overlay on the canvas to verify exactly which regions will change.

### 3. Pick and Confirm a Target Color

Available target colors depend on your active **Source Type**:

- **Classic LUT**: Pick directly from the calibrated colors included in that LUT.
- **Material Archive**: Enter or pick a desired shade, and Lumina calculates the closest reachable candidates that your selected filaments, layer count, and layer height can physically produce.

Selecting a candidate displays a pending source → target row. Verify both swatches and the highlighted canvas area, then confirm. Clicking **Cancel** on that pending row dismisses only the unconfirmed change without affecting earlier edits.

### 4. Review the Updated Previews

After confirming a replacement, check four things:

- **2D Preview**: Only the intended regions changed color;
- **3D Preview**: The 3D mesh has finished refreshing (give 3D geometry a moment if it finishes right after the 2D view);
- **Layer Preview**: The stacked filament sequence matches your updated color;
- **Exported 3MF**: Newly generated objects map cleanly to your physical spools in the slicer.

## Undo, Clear Remaps, and Cancel

- **Undo / Redo**: **Undo** reverts the most recent selection or replacement step, and **Redo** reapplies an undone action.
- **Clear Remaps**: Strips all global color remaps and local-region edits from the current session, restoring the base automatic match. Click **Generate** afterward if you want a fresh 3MF of the restored match.
- **Cancel (pending row)**: Discards only the uncommitted source → target pair currently awaiting confirmation.

## Working with Free Color

Clicking **Mark as Free Color** flags the selected matched color so that the next **Generate** exports its geometry as a standalone 3MF object (for example, `Free_FF0000`).

**When to use Free Color:**

- You want to map a logo, border, or flat accent directly to a dedicated spool in your slicer;
- You prefer to assign a specialty filament—such as silk, metallic, or glow-in-the-dark—outside the automatic layered color stack.

**How Free Color behaves:**

- It groups geometry by matched color, so every region sharing that matched color enters the same `Free_` object;
- Hardware slot limits still apply—make sure your printer or AMS has a slot ready for the spool you assign in your slicer;
- **Clear Free Colors** removes Free Color flags while leaving all color replacements and **Custom Recipes** untouched.

After clicking **Generate**, locate the `Free_FF0000` (or corresponding `Free_` hex) object in your slicer's object list and assign it to the physical filament spool you plan to load.

## Handling Gradient Groups

In **Material Archive** mode, smooth gradients can occasionally step across adjacent filament combinations, forming visible contour bands. When **Color Workstation** detects these patterns, it lists them as gradient groups:

- Click a group or sub-region first to inspect its highlight on the canvas;
- Merge a noisy sub-region or unify the group into a single representative shade when you want cleaner flat areas;
- Leave intentional shading, soft shadows, or skin-tone transitions unmerged when multi-step gradation looks better.

## Common Questions

### Why does clicking a swatch in the Palette do nothing?

Make sure **Preview** has completed first, and check which selection tool is active: **Pick blocks** and **Selection brush** select directly on the preview canvas, whereas **Same color** lets you select matched colors across the image.

### Why did the character's clothes change when I only meant to edit the eyes?

Both areas shared the same matched color while **Same color** was active. Click **Undo**, switch to **Pick blocks**, click only the eye regions, and confirm the highlight before applying the target color.

### Why doesn't the candidate color match the exact RGB hex I typed in?

**Material Archive** mode calculates colors achievable with your selected physical filaments, **Color Layers**, and **Layer Height**. Pick the closest reachable candidate and confirm critical shades with a small test print.

### Do I need to run Preview again after confirming a replacement?

Confirming a replacement refreshes the 2D, 3D, and **Layer Preview** views automatically. Once you are happy with the result, click **Generate** to export an updated 3MF.

### How does Free Color differ from color replacement?

Color replacement assigns a different reachable filament stack to your selected region. **Free Color** changes how 3MF meshes are split, exporting that matched color as its own object so you can assign a filament manually in your slicer.

### How can I save my color edits for later?

Export a recipe file via [Share Cards and JSON](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/share-card-json/) and store it alongside your original image, Material Archives or LUT, and final 3MF so you can reload and refine the project later.
