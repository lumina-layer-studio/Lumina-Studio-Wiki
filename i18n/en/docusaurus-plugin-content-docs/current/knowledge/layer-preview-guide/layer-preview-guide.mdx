# How to Read Lumina Layer Preview

Lumina's **main Preview** shows what surface color each area is predicted to produce once stacked filaments interact, while **Layer Preview** breaks down which filament color goes into every position on each individual color layer.

Reading both views together shows you both the finished look and the exact material stack behind every shade.

:::tip[Layer numbering order]
**Layer 1 is the outermost viewing face; higher numbers move inward toward the backing.** Layer Preview numbers optical recipe layers from the viewing surface inward, whereas your slicer counts physical Z-layers upward from the build plate.
:::

## How the three preview stages complement each other

| View | Primary question answered | What still needs verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lumina main Preview | What combined surface color will the material stack produce? | Physical spool selection, feeder slot mapping, and sliced toolpaths |
| Lumina Layer Preview | Which filament color occupies each position on every color layer? | Extrusion line width, seam placement, purge tower, supports, and print order |
| Slicer layer preview | Which exact extrusion paths will the nozzle lay down? | Optical color blending across translucent layers (predicted by Lumina's archive model) |

Once both Lumina views look clean, always inspect the sliced toolpaths in your slicer before sending the job to the printer.

## Step 1: Run Preview to load layer thumbnails

1. Upload your source image.
2. Select the Material Archives or Classic LUT you plan to print with.
3. Configure your modeling mode, dimensions, and color layers.
4. Click **Preview**.
5. Wait for the main Preview and the row of layer thumbnails underneath to finish loading.



<img

Layer thumbnails load automatically right after a valid Preview completes. If thumbnails are delayed, click **View Layers** to reload them on demand.

Whenever you change the source image, color source, color-layer count, Layer Height, or other core parameters, run **Preview** again so your layer masks reflect the updated recipe.

## Step 2: Read from viewing face to backing

Lumina arranges color layers from the front viewing surface inward:

- **Layer 1:** The outermost color layer right at the viewing face.
- **Middle layers:** Intermediate layers that transmit, filter, and blend light.
- **Last color layer:** The innermost color layer sitting directly against the structural backing.

<img
  src={require('./assets/02-read-layer-order.webp').default}
  alt="Five real Lumina layer-mask outputs arranged from Layer 1 viewing face to Layer 5 near the backing"
/>

Every thumbnail shows the **same artwork** sliced at a single optical recipe layer. When a shape is yellow in Layer 1 and blue in Layer 2, Lumina is stacking different filaments across depths to blend a new target color on the surface.

:::warning[Color layers vs. slicer Z layers]
Your slicer counts physical Z-layers upward from the build plate—including structural backing layers, the first layer, and any Relief height—whereas Lumina Layer Preview counts optical recipe layers inward from the viewing face. Also note that the physical backing usually spans multiple solid layers of base filament; the last Layer Preview thumbnail represents only the innermost color-recipe layer next to that backing.
:::

## Step 3: Check silhouette first, then color transitions

Scan the layer thumbnails in this order:

1. **Outer silhouette:** Are borders, interior cutouts, and transparent backgrounds crisp and consistent across layers?
2. **Fine details:** Do eyes, lettering, thin strokes, or small accents drop out unexpectedly on a single layer?
3. **Stack transitions:** Do color changes from layer to layer match your selected archive palette or custom recipe?
4. **Total layer count:** Does the number of thumbnails match your configured color layers or LUT?
5. **Recent edits:** Have any Color Workstation replacements or custom stack edits updated on the expected layers?

On a white page background, **white filament inside a small thumbnail can blend into the background**. Click any thumbnail to open the enlarged viewer: a gray-and-white checkerboard marks transparent space outside the model, making solid white filament inside the silhouette easy to spot.

## Step 4: Zoom in and step across adjacent layers

Click any layer thumbnail to open the full-size layer viewer:



<img
  src={require('./assets/03-zoom-and-compare.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina layer viewer with layer number, previous and next layer controls, zoom, pan, reset, and close instructions"
/>

Inside the viewer, you can:

- Click **Previous Layer** / **Next Layer** or press the `←` and `→` arrow keys.
- Scroll the mouse wheel to zoom in and out around the cursor.
- Click and drag to pan across fine borders and tiny color islands.
- Click **Reset Zoom** to fit the whole artwork back in view.
- Click `×`, press `Esc`, or click outside the modal to close it.

To spot subtle anomalies, zoom into a detail first and keep the viewport stationary while tapping `←` and `→`—stray single-layer specks, broken strokes, or sudden recipe jumps stand out immediately when the frame doesn't move.

## Step 5: Hover over the main Preview to inspect a single pixel

Rest the pointer anywhere on the main Preview for a moment to bring up the hover inspector card:

- **Magnifier loupe:** Zooms into the neighboring pixel grid around your pointer.
- **Pixel coordinates:** Lets you return to the exact same spot after editing a color or recipe.
- **Predicted color:** Shows the combined surface color and HEX value expected from the full stack.
- **Layers 1–N colors:** Lists the exact filament assigned to each optical layer at that pixel.

<img
  src={require('./assets/04-hover-read-recipe.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina hover inspector showing pixel coordinates, predicted colour, and each layer colour"
/>



It is completely normal for the predicted surface color to differ from every individual filament in the stack—Lumina models light transmission and opacity across layers rather than averaging HEX codes. Note that hover coordinates refer to image pixels in Preview rather than millimeter bed positions or feeder slot numbers.

## Step 6: Quick troubleshooting by symptom

| What you see | Where to check first |
| --- | --- |
| Main Preview colors already look off | Source image, Source Type, selected Material Archives / LUT, color count, and Modeling Mode |
| Main Preview looks fine, but one layer has stray specks | Compression noise in the source image, regional color replacements, or custom recipes |
| Every layer is missing the same section | Source alpha channel, crop bounds, unclosed SVG paths, or geometry settings |
| Layer count doesn't match expectations | Configured Color Layers, custom recipe layer expansion, or an outdated Preview |
| White regions look missing in thumbnails | Open the enlarged viewer and check solid white interior areas against the outer checkerboard |
| Layer thumbnails haven't updated after an edit | Wait a moment for automatic reload, or click **Preview** again if you changed core settings |
| Lumina layers look right, but slicer colors are scrambled | 3MF project import mode, project filaments, physical spools, and feeder slot mapping |
| Both Lumina and slicer match, yet the print shifts in color | Archive accuracy, filament batch, actual Layer Height, extrusion flow, and viewing light |

If the layer strip stays blank, gray, or stuck loading, test **Preview** with a small, simple image and default settings first; if it still fails, follow [The Page Will Not Open, Keeps Spinning, or Generation Fails](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/web-generation-troubleshooting/).

## Step 7: Verify physical toolpaths in your slicer

Because Layer Preview focuses on optical color recipes, open your exported 3MF as a full project in your slicer to verify physical printing details:

- Extrusion line widths and survival of thin text or fine strokes
- First-layer squish, adhesion, and seam placement
- Purge tower placement, bridges, and any required supports
- Mapping between project filaments and physical AMS, ACE, MMU, or external spools
- Bottom-to-top physical print order and filament swaps

Follow [Import a Lumina 3MF into the Slicer Correctly](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/) for the complete slicer check, and see [Why Lumina Preview, Slicer Preview, and the Print Differ](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/preview-slicer-print-differences/) if colors diverge between stages.

## Common questions





### Why is Layer 1 the viewing face instead of the first layer printed on the bed?

Layer Preview describes how light enters and reflects through the finished surface stack (starting at Layer 1 on the viewing face), whereas your slicer numbers physical Z-layers from the build plate upward for toolpath execution.

### Why doesn't the predicted color match any of the five layer filaments?

Translucent filaments filter and reflect light differently depending on layer thickness and stacking order, allowing a handful of base spools to blend dozens of intermediate surface shades.

### Why do some projects show fewer or more than five layers?

Total color layers depend on your configured Color Layers, Classic LUT specification, or custom stack recipes in Color Workstation. Both Layer Preview and your exported 3MF automatically adapt to the active layer count.

### Why is there still a View Layers button if thumbnails load automatically?

**View Layers** lets you manually request or refresh the layer masks anytime thumbnails are delayed over a slow connection.

### What does Transparent mean in the hover inspector?

Your cursor is hovering over empty canvas outside the model silhouette (or over an intentional cutout with no geometry). Glance at the magnifier loupe to see the nearest model edge.

### Does Layer Preview tell me which AMS slot to load?

No—Layer 1 through Layer N represent vertical optical layers inside the print rather than hardware feeder slots 1–4. Assign project filaments to your physical feeder slots inside the slicer after importing the 3MF.

### If small text is legible in Layer Preview, is it guaranteed to print?

Layer Preview confirms the text exists in the color data, but strokes narrower than your nozzle's extrusion width can still drop out during slicing. Always verify fine lettering and thin lines in your slicer's line-by-line toolpath preview.
