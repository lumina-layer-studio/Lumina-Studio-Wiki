# What Is the Difference Between Color Count, Free Color, Objects, and Material Slots?

From source image to finished print, the color and count numbers in Lumina Studio and your slicer describe distinct stages of the pipeline—and they do not need to match one another. For instance, a single illustration can be clustered into **48 quantized color groups**, mixed using **4 physical spools** stacked across **5 Color Layers**, exported as a 3MF with **more than 4 geometry objects**, and mapped to **4 AMS slots** at any slot positions you choose.

## Quick Reference: Eight Distinct Concepts

| Concept | What it defines | How it relates to other stages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source color | The original RGB value of each pixel in your image | Converted into printable layers through quantization and recipe matching |
| Quantize Colors | How many target color groups High-Fidelity clusters similar pixels into | Controls color grouping granularity, independent of spool count, AMS slots, or 3MF objects |
| Matched color & layered recipe | The reachable color and Z-layer sequence assigned to each target group | Mixes shades across thin layers so a handful of spools can produce dozens of colors |
| Selected Materials | Which Material Archives or Classic LUT channels participate in solving | Defines your active filament pool without requiring matching hardware slot numbers |
| Color Layers | Maximum number of stacked color-mixing layers per pixel along Z | Sets recipe depth; a single filament can repeat across multiple layers |
| Free Color | Exports a matched color's geometry as a separate `Free_HEX` 3MF object | Lets you assign a dedicated spool in your slicer, within your printer's slot capacity |
| 3MF object / part | Individual geometry meshes selectable in your slicer's object tree | Backing, outlines, loops, and Free Color regions form separate objects that can share filaments |
| Project filament, physical spool & machine slot | The 3MF filament entry, your real spool, and its hardware slot | Numbered independently—map each project filament to its real slot before printing |

## Quantize Colors: Grouping Continuous Pixels

Photographs, digital paintings, and anti-aliased artwork often contain thousands of subtle RGB variations. Rather than solving every unique pixel separately, **High-Fidelity** mode clusters nearby shades into a manageable set of target groups controlled by **Quantize Colors**:

- **Scope**: Applies to pixel quantization in **High-Fidelity** mode (Vector mode uses SVG shapes directly).
- **Range**: Standard slider values cover `8–256` plus **Auto Calculate**; unlocking the limit allows up to `8192` for extreme tonal artwork at higher memory and processing cost.
- **Effect**: Higher values preserve smoother gradient transitions, while lower values consolidate flat regions and speed up **Preview**.

Setting **Quantize Colors** to `48` groups the image into roughly 48 target shades, which are then reproduced using layered combinations of your selected filaments—you do not need 48 spools.

:::tip[When to Adjust]
If smooth gradients show visible stair-stepping, raise **Quantize Colors** moderately while keeping other settings fixed. If your artwork uses simple flat colors or **Preview** feels slow, lower the count.
:::

## Matched Colors and Layered Recipes

Once target groups are formed, Lumina matches each group to the closest color achievable with your selected Classic LUT or Material Archives. By varying the stacking order and repetition of thin semi-translucent layers, a small set of filaments produces a wide palette of visible shades.

With four Material Archives—white, red, yellow, and blue—and five **Color Layers**, different regions might stack:

- White → Red → White → White → White
- White → Yellow → Red → White → White
- White → Blue → Red → Yellow → White

All three recipes draw from the same four spools while producing distinct tints and hues. As a result, visible colors in your print routinely outnumber your loaded spools. If an entire hue family is missing from the preview, changing or adding a filament in your Material Archives or LUT will expand the gamut far more effectively than raising **Quantize Colors** alone. See [Material Archives or Classic LUT?](../material-profile-selection/material-profile-selection.mdx).

## Selected Materials vs. Color Layers

When **Source Type** is set to **Material Archive**, four controls divide the work cleanly:

- **Selected Materials**: The filament archives available to the color solver;
- **Base Plate Material**: The filament assigned to the structural backing;
- **Color Layers**: How many color-producing layers stack vertically per pixel;
- **Layer Height**: The physical thickness of each color layer.

Pairing four **Selected Materials** with five **Color Layers** is completely standard—any five-layer stack can reuse the same filament multiple times without needing a fifth spool.

Similarly, terms like 2-color, 4-color, 6-color, or 8-color in Classic LUT names indicate how many filament channels that LUT was calibrated with, rather than capping the number of visible shades on the print. Make sure your multi-material unit (or manual filament-swap plan) covers the active filament channels in your project.

## Free Color: Exporting an Independently Assignable Object

After running **Preview**, you can select any matched color in **Color Workstation** and click **Mark as Free Color**. On the next **Generate**, Lumina extracts that color's regions into a standalone 3MF object named from its hex code (for example, `Free_FF0000`).

**Common uses:**

- Assigning a specific specialty or brand-color spool to a logo, border, or lettering;
- Selecting and remapping a flat region directly in your slicer's object list;
- Isolating silk, metallic, or glow-in-the-dark filament from standard color-mixing layers.

Whenever you mark or clear a Free Color, click **Generate** again to export an updated 3MF, then assign that `Free_FF0000` object to your chosen project filament and physical slot in the slicer.

:::warning[Physical Filament Determines Color]
Free Color creates an independent object for manual assignment in your slicer. The printed color depends on the physical spool you load, filament settings, build plate texture, and lighting.
:::

## Objects, Project Filaments, Spools, and Slots

Inside your slicer, verify material assignments across four distinct levels:

### 1. Objects and Parts

Objects are individual 3D meshes. Backing plates, per-material meshes, outlines, keychain loops, coatings, and Free Color regions all export as separate objects—so seeing more objects than filaments is normal, and multiple objects can share the same project filament.

### 2. Project Filaments

Project filaments are the numbered filament definitions embedded inside the 3MF. The badge next to each object indicates which project filament it uses, and its on-screen swatch serves as a visual label.

### 3. Physical Spools and Machine Slots

Your physical spool is the real filament roll on hand, and the machine slot is where that roll currently sits in your AMS, ACE, MMU, or external spool holder.

Follow this check order before printing:

1. Confirm that every object in the tree points to the right project filament;
2. Match each project filament to the exact brand, material type, and color spool you plan to print with;
3. Map each project filament to the hardware slot holding that spool (for example, mapping Project Filament 3 to AMS Slot A1);
4. Slice the plate and inspect the display surface layer by layer in Preview.

For a full walkthrough, see [Open a Lumina 3MF in Your Slicer](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).

## Which Setting Should You Adjust?

| Goal | What to adjust | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smooth out visible banding in gradients | Raise **Quantize Colors** | Adds finer target color groups without adding physical spools |
| Expand a missing hue range across the image | Change **Material Archives / LUT** or **Selected Materials** | Gamut limits come from your chosen filaments rather than quantization |
| Increase vertical color-mixing combinations | Adjust **Color Layers** | Balance finer tonal steps against model thickness and print time |
| Print a specific region with a dedicated spool | **Mark as Free Color** and **Generate** again | Assign the exported `Free_` object to your target spool in the slicer |
| Manage a long list of objects in the slicer | Check **object-to-filament assignments** | Multiple structural and color meshes routinely share one project filament |
| Align project filaments with different AMS slots | Remap slots in your slicer's **Send Print** dialog | No need to physically rearrange spools just to match slot numbers |

## Two Practical Examples

### Example 1: 48 Quantized Colors, 4 Spools, 5 Color Layers

An illustration uses `48` **Quantize Colors**, four Material Archives (white, red, yellow, and blue), and `5` **Color Layers**:

- Lumina clusters the image into up to 48 target color groups;
- Each group receives a recipe up to five layers deep drawn from those four filaments, requiring only four feeder slots;
- Enabling **Separate Backing** or **Outline** adds extra geometry objects in the 3MF while still sharing the same four project filaments.

### Example 2: Marking a Red Logo as Free Color

After **Preview**, you select the matched red on a logo, click **Mark as Free Color**, and click **Generate** again:

- **Quantize Colors** stays unchanged;
- The new 3MF includes a dedicated `Free_` mesh for that logo region;
- In your slicer, you assign that object to your dedicated red spool (making sure an AMS slot is available if it introduces an extra filament) and slice the plate.

## Common Questions

### Does setting Quantize Colors to 256 require 256 AMS slots?

No. `256` sets the ceiling on target color clusters in **High-Fidelity** mode—all clusters are mixed from the small set of physical filaments you selected.

### Why can I select eight filaments when Color Layers is set to 5?

**Selected Materials** sets the pool of available filaments, whereas **Color Layers** sets how many layers stack vertically at any single pixel. Each 5-layer recipe picks the best combination from your 8-filament pool.

### Are Classic 4-color, 6-color, or 8-color LUTs the same thing as Free Color?

No. Those numbers describe how many base filament channels a Classic LUT uses. **Free Color** extracts a specific matched color into its own 3MF object so you can assign a filament manually in your slicer.

### Why does my slicer list more objects than the four filaments I picked?

Structural parts—such as separated backing, outlines, loops, transparent coatings, and Free Color regions—export as distinct meshes while sharing your project filaments.

### Does Project Filament 2 have to sit in AMS Slot 2?

No. Project filament numbers and physical hardware slots are independent; simply map Project Filament 2 to whichever AMS slot holds that spool when sending the print.

### My slicer swatch looks slightly different from my real spool. Will it print the wrong color?

On-screen swatches are approximate identifiers. As long as the chain from object → project filament → physical spool → machine slot is mapped accurately, the printer will feed the right spool.
