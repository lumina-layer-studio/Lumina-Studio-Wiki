# Manage Material Archives: Edit, Re-Verify, Import, and Export

A Lumina Material Archive stores the calibrated optical profile for a specific physical spool of filament. Inside the Material Library, you can inspect archive metadata, tidy up display names and swatches, review past calibration runs, merge new verification data, and back up or transfer archives as ZIP packages.

Keep this core distinction in mind:

- **Edit Details** updates display labels and the identification swatch shown in the interface.
- **Re-Verify** updates measured optical parameters through a new gradient-card calibration run.

Adjusting a swatch color only changes how the card appears in your library—it never recalculates light transmission or fitting parameters.

## Gradient-card ZIP or material archive ZIP?

| Package type | Where you get it | Where to import it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gradient-card kit ZIP | Generated and downloaded from **Gradient Card Generation** | Import in **Gradient Card Extraction** alongside photos of your printed white- and black-backed cards to build an archive |
| Material Archive ZIP | Exported from the **Material Library** or shared by another user | Click **Import Material ZIP** in the **Material Library** to install finished archives |

Although both files end in `.zip`, they hold different data for different stages of the workflow and cannot be swapped. To create an archive from scratch, see [Generate and Print Gradient Calibration Cards](../../tutorials/gradient-card-generate-print/gradient-card-generate-print.mdx) and [Photograph and Extract Gradient Cards](../../tutorials/gradient-card-extract-profile/gradient-card-extract-profile.mdx).



## Step 1: Browse brand groups and selection controls



Open **Material Management → Material Library**.

<img
  src={require('./assets/01-library-overview.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Material Library with selection count, brand groups, Select All, import, export, and refresh controls highlighted"
/>

The header bar shows your current selection count alongside total installed archives (export remains disabled until at least one archive is checked). Brand groups start collapsed by default, with a badge indicating how many archives belong to each brand:

- **Select All:** Selects every archive currently in the library.
- **Import Material ZIP:** Installs one or more exported Lumina archive ZIP packages.
- **Export Selected:** Downloads all checked archives together in a single ZIP file.
- **Refresh:** Reloads the library after importing or external file updates.

Expand a brand group to inspect individual cards rather than relying on color names alone—similar names can belong to different product lines, finishes, or layer heights.

## Step 2: Check archive details before use



<img
  src={require('./assets/02-material-card.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Material Archive card with material name, brand, directory, display colour, fit error, verification count, usage count, and layer height highlighted"
/>

Review these fields on a Material Archive card before selecting, editing, or deleting it:

- **Material name and brand:** Human-readable labels for searching and grouping.
- **Directory identity and source:** The internal folder ID that uniquely identifies the archive.
- **Display color and HEX code:** Visual swatch for quick recognition in lists.
- **Layer Height:** The print layer height used when the gradient cards were calibrated.
- **Fit error and verification count:** Optical fitting accuracy and number of merged calibration runs.
- **Created and last-updated timestamps:** Helpful for telling apart older and newer revisions.



Note that **Usage Count** simply tracks how many times you've used the archive in conversions rather than scoring calibration quality. When choosing among similarly colored cards, match the exact filament product, polymer family, batch (if known), and Layer Height—see [Choose Material Archives or a Classic LUT](../material-profile-selection/material-profile-selection.mdx).

## Step 3: Edit display labels and swatches

Click **Edit Details** on a card to tidy up how it appears in your library:



<img
  src={require('./assets/03-edit-dialog.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Edit Material Details dialog with name, brand, display colour, directory identity, and non-recalibration warning highlighted"
/>

Here you can update:

- **Material Name**
- **Brand** (optional)
- **Display Color** swatch used for UI identification

Saving updates these labels while keeping the underlying directory ID and full verification history intact—handy for fixing typos, standardizing brand names, or making similar swatches easier to tell apart.

:::warning[Display color vs. calibration data]
**Edit Details** does not reprocess gradient-card photos or recalculate optical parameters (`E`, `K`, or `C0`). If physical prints shift in hue or brightness, check your spool identity, lighting setup, and Layer Height, and run **Re-Verify** to update the measured model.
:::

## Step 4: Inspect verification history

Click **Show Verification History** to review every calibration run logged for the archive, listed newest first:

<img
  src={require('./assets/04-verification-history.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina verification history showing several dated runs, display colour, fit error, RMSE, and diagnostic values"
/>

For routine checks, focus on:

- **Date and timestamp** of each calibration run
- **Estimated display color** stability across runs
- **Fit error and linear RMSE** under consistent lighting and camera settings
- **Layer Height** recorded with the run

Card orientation, `E`, `K`, and white/black `C0` values serve as deeper diagnostics when troubleshooting glare, uneven lighting, or corner-alignment issues. Evaluate fit numbers alongside consistent lighting and a physical test swatch rather than picking a run by the lowest error number alone.

## Step 5: Re-verify an existing archive





Click **Re-Verify** on a card whenever you want to refine or update its optical measurements—for example, after improving your lighting setup, opening a fresh batch of the same filament, or adding another independent sample run:

<img
  src={require('./assets/05-reverify-target.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Material Re-Verification flow with target archive, current verification count, ZIP upload, white and black backing images, and Merge Results highlighted"
/>

Confirm the target archive banner at the top of the extraction page, then:

1. Import the matching gradient-card kit ZIP for that exact spool and Layer Height.
2. Upload your newly photographed white-backed and black-backed gradient cards.
3. Verify card orientation, even illumination, and corner markers.
4. Run extraction and click **Merge Results** to append the new calibration run to the archive (or cancel if you selected the wrong card or files).

Only merge runs printed from the same filament product and Layer Height so the archive's optical model stays consistent.



## Step 6: Export a ZIP backup before major changes



Check one or more archive cards and click **Export Selected**:

<img
  src={require('./assets/06-import-export.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Material Library after exporting one selected archive as a ZIP"
/>

Exported Material Archive ZIPs are ideal for:

- Saving an offline backup before deleting or reorganizing archives
- Migrating your calibrated library to another authorized computer
- Sharing custom calibration profiles for the exact same filament and Layer Height
- Snapshotting a known-good archive before merging a new verification run

Because deleting an archive in the Material Library cannot be undone, always save an exported ZIP outside Lumina's working folder first.

## Step 7: Import Material Archive ZIPs

Click **Import Material ZIP** to install one or more Lumina archive packages at once. To protect existing work, the importer never overwrites local archives destructively: identical package revisions are skipped automatically, while directory name collisions with different data are saved side by side under a suffixed directory ID.

After importing, expand the brand group and verify:

- Displayed material name, brand, and directory ID
- Verification count and calibrated Layer Height
- Source provenance and usage rights (if shared by a third party)
- A quick physical test swatch before starting a large print



Always import the intact `.zip` package directly rather than unzipping and repacking internal JSON files by hand.

## Recommended local backup layout

Keeping dated backups alongside brief calibration notes makes long-term library maintenance effortless:

```text
material-archives/
  YYYY-MM-DD/
    exported-materials.zip
    verification-notes.txt
    reference-photos/
```



Record the exact filament line, batch code, Layer Height, nozzle size, and lighting setup in your notes, and strip any personal machine paths or account details before sharing archives publicly.

## Common questions









### Why doesn't changing Display Color alter my printed colors?

**Display Color** only sets the UI badge swatch in the Material Library. Color conversion relies on the archive's measured optical parameters—use **Re-Verify** and print a test swatch whenever physical color predictions need updating.

### Does a lower fit error guarantee a more accurate print?

Fit error is a helpful comparison metric when lighting, camera exposure, and card alignment stay controlled. Specular glare, color casts, or mismatched cards can still skew real-world accuracy even when numerical error looks low, so always confirm with a physical swatch.

### Is it safe to rename an archive?

Yes. **Edit Details** updates the visible name and brand while keeping the internal directory ID unchanged. Avoid giving multiple distinct archives identical display names so they remain easy to tell apart in conversion pickers.

### Can I use a Material Archive shared by another maker right away?

Verify the exact filament brand, product line, finish, calibrated Layer Height, and sharing permissions first, then print a small validation swatch on your own setup before committing to a full-size artwork.

### What should I check before deleting a duplicate archive?

Export a backup ZIP first, compare directory IDs, timestamps, and verification histories between the two cards, and make sure no active project relies on the copy you plan to remove.

### Why did an imported archive land under a different brand group?

Brand grouping reads the brand string stored inside the archive's metadata. Open **Edit Details** and update the **Brand** field to move it into your preferred brand group without touching its calibration data.
