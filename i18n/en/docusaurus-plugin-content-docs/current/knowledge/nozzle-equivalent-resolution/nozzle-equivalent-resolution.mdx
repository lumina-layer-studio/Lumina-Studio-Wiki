Using standard Bambu Studio default line widths, a **0.4 mm nozzle provides roughly 60 PPI**, and a **0.2 mm nozzle provides roughly 115 PPI**.

Because an FDM printer extrudes continuous plastic toolpaths with real physical width, the slicer's **line width** determines how much flat detail fits into a given print size. Treating one reference line width as one sampling interval gives you a practical way to estimate detail capacity before printing.

:::tip[Key takeaway]
At the same physical dimensions, a 0.2 mm nozzle fits nearly twice as many lines across each edge—giving facial features, small text, fine textures, and isolated color patches far more room to stay distinct.
:::

## Why calculate from line width instead of nozzle diameter?

Nozzle diameter and extruded line width measure two different things:

- **Nozzle diameter**: the physical orifice size and hardware preset baseline (`0.2 mm`, `0.4 mm`).
- **Line width**: the target width of extruded plastic pressed onto the build plate or underlying layer.
- **Equivalent pixel**: a practical approximation that treats one reference line width as one planar sample.

In standard Bambu Studio presets, a 0.4 mm nozzle defaults to a `0.42 mm` line width and a 0.2 mm nozzle defaults to `0.22 mm`. Slicers also assign separate widths to outer walls, inner walls, first layers, top surfaces, and infill—and with the Arachne variable-width wall generator, local widths adapt dynamically to narrow features. Using your preset's reference line width gives a closer estimate than dividing by the nominal orifice size.

:::note[Use actual line width]
A 0.2 mm nozzle profile does not extrude lines that are literally 0.20 mm wide by default. If you customize line widths in your slicer preset, substitute your configured line width into the formulas below.
:::

## Calculating equivalent resolution for your print

Divide any finished edge length by your slicer's line width to estimate how many reference samples fit along that edge:

```text
Equivalent samples on one side ≈ side length (mm) / actual line width (mm)
```

For a 100 mm tall print:

```text
0.4 mm nozzle: 100 / 0.42 ≈ 238
0.2 mm nozzle: 100 / 0.22 ≈ 455
```

That gives roughly 238 or 455 reference samples vertically; for an 80 mm width, divide `80` by the same line width to find the horizontal count.

To convert directly into equivalent pixels per inch (PPI):

```text
Equivalent PPI ≈ 25.4 / actual line width (mm)

0.4 mm nozzle: 25.4 / 0.42 ≈ 60 PPI
0.2 mm nozzle: 25.4 / 0.22 ≈ 115 PPI
```

Switching from 0.4 mm to 0.2 mm nearly doubles linear sample density along each axis—roughly `3.6×` across two dimensions—while actual visual sharpness also depends on toolpath routing, color contrast, and extrusion behavior.

## What 60, 100, 150, and 200 mm look like in practice

| Side length | 0.4 mm nozzle (0.42 mm width) | 0.2 mm nozzle (0.22 mm width) | Practical scale |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 60 mm | about 143 | about 273 | Small fridge magnet: ~144p vs. ~270p along one edge |
| 100 mm | about 238 | about 455 | Coaster / palm-sized piece: ~240p vs. ~480p |
| 150 mm | about 357 | about 682 | Postcard height: ~360p vs. ~720p |
| 200 mm | about 476 | about 909 | Short edge of A4 paper: ~480p vs. ~900p |

Labels like `144p`, `240p`, and `480p` borrow familiar video line counts solely to illustrate one-dimensional sample counts along a single edge (printed toolpaths form curved plastic lines with surface texture rather than glowing square pixels):

- **60 mm** (fridge magnets, keychains): tight dimensions make the jump from ~143 to ~273 lines immediately noticeable.
- **100 mm** (coasters, desk miniatures): ~238 vs. ~455 lines—pick based on how intricate your artwork is and how long you want to print.
- **150 mm** (postcard size): both nozzles work well; the main trade-off is fine-line retention versus print time.
- **200 mm** (short side of A4): a 0.4 mm nozzle already fits ~476 lines vertically, plenty for bold illustrations, while 0.2 mm (~909 lines) excels on intricate linework.

## Other factors that shape fine detail

### Source image quality

Clean silhouettes, crisp color regions, and clear contrast matter more than raw megapixel count. Compression blocks, sensor noise, or blurry edges in the source image cannot be recovered by switching to a smaller nozzle.

### Modeling mode

Pixel Art mode aligns geometry to a regular grid, whereas High-Fidelity and Vector modes trace color regions into smooth, continuous curves—so diagonal lines and arcs print smoothly without stair-stepping.

### Wall generator

Classic wall generation stays close to fixed reference widths. Arachne varies extrusion width locally to squeeze into narrow tapers and thin strokes, helping preserve extra detail even on a 0.4 mm nozzle within physical extrusion limits.

### First layer, flow, and build plate

When the viewing face prints face-down against the build plate, first-layer line width, flow ratio, and plate texture directly define surface crispness. Over-squishing causes adjacent colors to bleed into each other, while under-extrusion leaves tiny pinholes.

### Filament opacity and color contrast

High-contrast boundaries (such as dark outlines against white) read sharply to the eye, whereas tiny details between two similarly toned translucent filaments blend together more softly.

### Viewing distance

Keychains and 60–100 mm magnets are held inches from the eye, making fine detail critical. Wall art at 200 mm or larger is typically viewed from arm's length or across a room, where a 0.4 mm print looks crisp and balanced.

## Choosing between 0.2 mm and 0.4 mm

- **Choose a 0.2 mm nozzle** for compact pieces (60–100 mm) or artwork packed with small eyes, lettering, thin outlines, and tiny isolated color islands, when you are comfortable with longer print times.
- **Choose a 0.4 mm nozzle** for medium-to-large prints, bold graphic art, faster turnaround, and everyday reliability with standard printer setups.

:::tip[Choosing a nozzle]
Slice your project and inspect the eyes, lettering, thin lines, and smallest color regions in the slicer's layer preview. If all critical details generate clean toolpaths with your current nozzle, you are ready to print; if fine features drop out or merge, step down to a 0.2 mm nozzle or scale up the print dimensions.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 0.2 mm nozzle equal to 127 PPI?

`127 PPI` (`25.4 / 0.2`) divides by the bare nozzle orifice. Because extruded plastic spreads slightly wider than the orifice, calculating with the `0.22 mm` default line width yields a more realistic `115 PPI`.

### Will a higher-resolution source image always print sharper?

Not beyond your physical line-width limit. Sub-line-width details merge during modeling or slicing, and noisy high-resolution images can introduce unwanted speckles. Clean edges and strong contrast make a bigger difference.

### Why doesn't a 0.2 mm print look literally twice as sharp?

“Nearly twice” refers to linear toolpath count along each axis. Perceived sharpness also depends on source detail, color contrast, first-layer squish, and viewing distance—bold artwork with large flat regions will look similar on both nozzles.

### Can a 0.4 mm nozzle print sharp large-format art?

Yes. Scaling up increases how many toolpaths fit across the image: a 200 mm tall print on a 0.4 mm nozzle has roughly 476 vertical sample positions, which works well for posters, cartoons, and wall decor viewed from normal distances.

### Does layer height change XY equivalent resolution?

Layer height governs Z-axis stacking thickness and color blending rather than XY line count, though first-layer height and flow do affect how wide the bottom lines spread against the build plate.

### Can Arachne print features narrower than the default line width?

Yes, locally. Arachne narrows or widens individual walls to fit tapered corners and thin strokes that fixed-width walls would drop, within the physical flow limits of your nozzle and filament.
