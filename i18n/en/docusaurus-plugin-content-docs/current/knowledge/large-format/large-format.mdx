# Large Format: Tile Size, Assembly Order, and ZIP Files

Large Format splits a single oversized artwork into a grid of adjacent tiles, generates an independent 3MF file for each tile, and bundles them into a single ZIP archive—ideal for murals, signage, display boards, and multi-panel wall art larger than a single build plate.

Key points at a glance:

- **Assembled size vs. tile limit:** **Width** and **Height** set the dimensions of the finished, assembled piece, while **Tile Width** and **Tile Height** cap the maximum size of any single 3MF tile.
- **Row and column naming:** Tiles are numbered top to bottom and left to right, starting with `R1C1` at the top-left corner.
- **Remainder edges:** Whenever the assembled size doesn't divide evenly by the tile limit, the rightmost column and bottom row hold the smaller remainder dimensions.
- **Butt-joint boundaries:** Tiles meet along clean, non-overlapping straight cuts without automatic puzzle tabs, alignment pins, or glue-gap offsets.
- **Per-tile slicer verification:** Preview displays the full artwork for color and layer inspection; after downloading the ZIP, open each 3MF in your slicer to verify bed fit, filament mapping, and toolpaths.

The walkthrough below uses a `450×300 mm` artwork with a `180×180 mm` tile limit, producing a `3×2` grid of six 3MF files.

## When to use Large Format

Large Format works well when:

- Your finished artwork exceeds your printer's usable build plate area.
- Every tile shares the same color source, structural settings, and color Layer Height.
- You plan to align and mount the printed panels onto a backing board or frame manually.
- You are ready to slice, label, and inspect each tile individually.

Use a different approach if each panel needs its own palette or thickness, or if you want interlocking puzzle tabs (use Puzzle mode or add custom joints in an external CAD tool instead). Before tiling a brand-new image or palette, run a quick small-scale single-image test to confirm your colors and layer structure first.

## Step 1: Enter the assembled dimensions

Upload your artwork, select your color source, and enter the final **Width** or **Height** of the complete assembled piece (with aspect ratio locked, the second dimension updates automatically).

Ensure your framing and orientation are finalized before tiling—see [Upload, Replace, Crop, and Rotate an Image](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/image-upload-crop-rotate/) if you need to crop or rotate the source image first.

## Step 2: Enable Large Format and set tile limits

<img
  src={require('./assets/01-large-format-settings.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Studio interface with finished size, Large Format Mode, tile limits, and a 3 by 2 calculation highlighted"
/>

Turn on **Large Format Mode**, then enter **Tile Width** and **Tile Height** as maximum per-tile dimensions.

In this example:

- **Assembled size:** `450×300 mm`
- **Tile limit:** `180×180 mm`
- **Columns:** `ceil(450 ÷ 180) = 3`
- **Rows:** `ceil(300 ÷ 180) = 2`
- **Total tiles:** `3 × 2 = 6`

The readout `3×2 = 6 (90×120mm)` shows the total grid count alongside the remainder width of the last column (`90 mm`) and remainder height of the bottom row (`120 mm`). Here are the exact dimensions of all six tiles:

| Tile coordinate | Physical dimensions |
| --- | --- |
| R1C1, R1C2 | 180×180 mm |
| R1C3 | 90×180 mm |
| R2C1, R2C2 | 180×120 mm |
| R2C3 | 90×120 mm |

Base your tile limits on your printer's **usable** printable area rather than its nominal bed size, leaving clearance for excluded bed corners, purge lines, bed clips, Brims, or outer borders. Verify a safe maximum footprint in your slicer beforehand.

## Step 3: Read the tile coordinate map

<img
  src={require('./assets/02-tile-map.webp').default}
  alt="Concept diagram showing a 450 by 300 millimetre artwork split into R1C1 through R2C3"
/>

Each exported filename encodes its grid position:

- `R` (Row) counts downward from top to bottom.
- `C` (Column) counts across from left to right.
- `R1C1` sits at the upper-left corner, and `R2C3` sits at the lower-right corner in this `3×2` layout.

Keep the original coordinate filenames throughout slicing, and jot the coordinate (`R1C1`, `R1C2`, etc.) on masking tape on the back of each printed panel right as it comes off the bed so similar-looking tiles never get mixed up.

:::warning[No automatic seam joints]
Large Format slices straight along adjacent tile edges without adding overlap lips, registration pins, puzzle tabs, or shrinkage offsets. If your installation requires mechanical alignment features, build them into the artwork or an external 3D modeling tool beforehand.
:::

## Step 5: Generate and save the ZIP archive

Start generation and wait for all tiles to finish packaging without refreshing the page or re-uploading files while the job is running.

<img
  src={require('./assets/04-download-zip.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Studio result with six generated Large Format tiles and Download ZIP highlighted"
/>

In our example, the completed job outputs a ~16.9 MB ZIP archive containing six 3MF files:

```text
large-format-demo_R1C1.3mf
large-format-demo_R1C2.3mf
large-format-demo_R1C3.3mf
large-format-demo_R2C1.3mf
large-format-demo_R2C2.3mf
large-format-demo_R2C3.3mf
```

:::warning[Check for missing tiles]
If a complex job only finishes a subset of tiles, newer builds still let you download the completed tiles while flagging any missing coordinates. Check the status notice, total file count, and `R/C` sequence so every missing tile is regenerated before you begin printing.
:::

Right after downloading, extract the archive to a permanent project folder and confirm that:

- The 3MF file count matches your expected grid total.
- Every row and column coordinate is present without gaps.
- Each file extracts cleanly and has a valid non-zero file size.

Because browser download links expire when you refresh or start a new session, always save your extracted 3MF files locally right away.

## Step 6: Inspect every tile in your slicer

Open each 3MF as a project in your slicer and check:

- Part dimensions in millimeters match the expected tile table.
- Geometry sits comfortably inside the printable area with no out-of-bounds warnings.
- Viewing face, backing orientation, and Z-rotation are consistent across every tile.
- Printer preset, nozzle diameter, Layer Height, and feeder slot mappings are accurate.
- Sliced color toolpaths are continuous, with clean outer seam edges and no unwanted Brim widening the mating borders.

For uniform gloss and minimal dimensional drift between panels, slice and print every tile with the same printer profile, nozzle, Layer Height, filament batch, build plate texture, and part orientation. For slot assignment details, see [Import a Lumina 3MF into the Slicer Correctly](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).

## Step 7: Dry-fit before final mounting

Lay out the printed tiles on a flat table before applying any tape, glue, or fasteners: arrange Row 1 from `R1C1` rightward, add subsequent rows underneath, and verify pattern continuity and edge flushness (lightly deburring any first-layer elephant foot if needed). When mounting to a backboard or frame, anchor one reference corner first and work outward row by row so tiny tolerances don't compound.

Choose adhesives, mounting tape, or mechanical hardware suited to your filament polymer, panel weight, and indoor/outdoor environment, and test bond strength beforehand for wall-mounted pieces.

## Common questions

### Why is the bottom-right tile only 90×120 mm?

`180×180 mm` sets the upper limit per tile. Subtracting two `180 mm` columns from the `450 mm` total width leaves `90 mm` for Column 3, and subtracting one `180 mm` row from the `300 mm` total height leaves `120 mm` for Row 2.

### Can I make every tile the exact same size?

Yes—choose assembled Width and Height values that are exact multiples of your Tile Width and Tile Height (or pad your source canvas with intentional margins), taking care not to stretch the artwork out of proportion.

### Does Lumina add overlap lips or alignment pins?

No. Tiles are cut flush along shared borders. If you need lap joints, dowel holes, or cutting allowances, build them into your source design or CAD model ahead of time and factor their extra width into your bed-size limit.

### Full Preview looked fine—why does one tile exceed my build plate in the slicer?

Lumina's 2D Preview displays the whole artwork rather than machine-specific bed keep-out zones, purge strips, or slicer Brims. Disable Brims along mating edges, reposition the part on the plate, or reduce Tile Width and Tile Height by a few millimeters and regenerate.

### One 3MF tile failed to open. Do I have to redo the whole project?

Keep the ZIP and the working tiles you already extracted, then run generation again with the same settings. If the exact same coordinate fails repeatedly, inspect that area of the artwork for micro-fragments or complex SVG effects, and consult [Slicer Detection and 3MF Open Troubleshooting](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/slicer-path-and-open-troubleshooting/).
