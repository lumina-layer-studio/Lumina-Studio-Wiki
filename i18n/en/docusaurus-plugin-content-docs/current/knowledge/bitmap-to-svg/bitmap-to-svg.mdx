# Convert a Bitmap to SVG, Then Continue in Image Converter

Lumina's **Vectorizer** traces bitmap images—PNG, JPG, WebP, or BMP—into clean SVG paths so logos, icons, line art, and flat-color illustrations can scale smoothly and use the native Vector workflow.

Because automatic tracing fits new curves to existing pixels, it works best on artwork with clear edges rather than photographs or soft gradients, and it cannot recover detail that is missing or blurred in the original image.

## Check Whether Tracing Fits Your Artwork

**Good candidates for tracing:**

- Logos, icons, signs, and symbols;
- Line art with crisp boundaries;
- Flat-color illustrations with a limited palette;
- Outlined lettering and clear geometric shapes.

**Better kept as a bitmap (or cleaned up before tracing):**

- Photographs and natural textures;
- Soft drop shadows and continuous gradients;
- Tiny or heavily compressed JPG files;
- Transparent edges with gray or white halos;
- Pixel art or noisy scans full of isolated specks.

If your file is already a native SVG, upload it directly to **Image Converter** instead of tracing it again. For background on both formats, see [Bitmap vs Vector Images](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/bitmap-vs-vector/).

## Step 1: Upload the Bitmap and Run Defaults

Open **Vectorizer** and upload a PNG, JPG, WebP, or BMP file while keeping a backup of the original image.

Start with a baseline pass:

1. Leave **Color Count** set to **Auto**;
2. Keep **Smoothness** and **Detail Level** at their default values;
3. Click **Start Vectorization** and wait for the preview to finish.

Running defaults first gives you a clear reference point before adjusting individual sliders.

## Step 2: Compare the Original and SVG Previews

<img
  src={require('./assets/02-compare-result.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Vectorizer source and SVG result comparison with text, thin lines, corners, and colour regions called out"
/>

Inspect these key areas side by side:

- **Text and small symbols**: Check that letter counters and enclosed loops stay open.
- **Thin lines**: Confirm that narrow strokes stay continuous and even.
- **Sharp corners**: Make sure crisp points have not been overly rounded or clipped.
- **Closed color regions**: Look for pinholes, color bleed, or stray islands.
- **Transparent borders**: Check that empty background areas did not turn into a solid rectangle.
- **Gradients**: Check whether banded color steps look clean at your intended print size.

Zoom in on the smallest feature that matters in your print, keeping in mind that features narrower than your nozzle's extrusion width at the final millimeter scale still cannot be printed physically.

:::tip[Change one setting at a time]
If edges look jagged, adjust **Smoothness** or **Detail Level** one step at a time and compare again. If the trace produces hundreds of tiny stray paths, clean up background noise in the bitmap or lower **Color Count** rather than increasing color steps.
:::

## Step 3: Download the SVG or Send It to Image Converter

The result summary displays the generated shape count, color count, and palette swatches. Keep in mind that a higher shape count reflects greater path complexity rather than sharper detail.

Choose how to proceed:

- **Download SVG**: Saves the traced file locally so you can archive a copy or refine nodes, paths, and fills in an external vector editor.
- **Send to Image Converter**: Loads the traced SVG directly into Lumina's converter and switches **Modeling Mode** to **Vector**.

## Step 4: Finish Modeling in Vector Mode

  src={require('./assets/04-vector-mode.webp').default}

Once **Send to Image Converter** loads your traced SVG in **Vector** mode, complete the conversion workflow:

1. Select **Source Type** and choose your Classic LUT or Material Archives;
2. Set the finished millimeter dimensions, **Backing Thickness**, and structure mode;
3. Click **Preview** to inspect matched color regions and layered recipes;
4. Click **Generate** to export the 3MF, then open the full project in your slicer to verify layer toolpaths.

## Troubleshooting Common Trace Issues

| Symptom | Recommended fix |
| --- | --- |
| A solid white background appears | Remove the background or crop empty canvas margins before tracing |
| Letter counters or small holes fill in | Start from a higher-resolution source, use bolder type, or clean up paths in a vector editor |
| Thin lines vanish | Thicken strokes in the source artwork and check nozzle line-width limits at print size |
| Hundreds of tiny stray shapes | Clean up JPG compression artifacts and specks, or reduce **Color Count** |
| Smooth gradients split into visible bands | Expected for flat vector regions; switch to the original bitmap in **High-Fidelity** mode for smooth tonal transitions |
| SVG loads with holes or missing fills | Check path closure, compound paths, masks, and fill rules |
| Tracing takes a long time | Crop tightly and avoid tracing photographic textures or heavy noise |

For deeper SVG diagnostics, see [SVG Upload, White Background, Holes, and Missing Detail](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/svg-troubleshooting/).

## Common Questions

### Does vectorization increase the resolution of a blurry image?

Tracing replaces pixel grids with mathematical curves that scale smoothly without blocky edges, but it cannot reconstruct facial features, fine lettering, or textures that were already blurred in the source bitmap.

### Should I set Color Count as high as possible?

No. Extra colors often split anti-aliased edges and compression noise into fragile slivers. Use the lowest **Color Count** that captures the intended palette cleanly.

### Why does a traced photo look posterized?

Vector tracing represents continuous tone using discrete flat-color shapes. Unless you want a stylized poster effect, keep photographs as bitmaps and use **High-Fidelity** mode.

### Can I edit the downloaded SVG in another app before converting?

Yes. Click **Download SVG**, edit paths and fills in Illustrator, Inkscape, or Affinity Designer, and upload the saved SVG to **Image Converter**. Stick to standard closed paths and solid fills—external fonts, blur filters, complex masks, and embedded rasters may not convert into 3D geometry.

### Why does a clean detail in the SVG disappear after slicing?

A vector shape can be mathematically sharp yet narrower than your nozzle's minimum line width at the target print dimensions. Always check fine details in your slicer's Layer Preview.

### Should I trace pixel art into SVG?

Keep pixel art as a bitmap and select **Pixel Art** mode instead—it preserves crisp grid cells directly without rounding or merging corners. See [Choose High-Fidelity, Pixel Art, or Vector Mode](pathname:///en/docs/knowledge/image-mode-selection/).
