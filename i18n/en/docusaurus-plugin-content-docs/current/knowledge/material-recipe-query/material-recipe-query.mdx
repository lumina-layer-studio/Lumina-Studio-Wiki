# Build Material Stacks, Predict Color, and Print Validation Swatches

**Material Recipe Query** estimates the surface color of a custom filament stack built from your Material Archives. Using a forward optical model, you select the filament layers, substrate, and color-layer height, compare predicted colors side by side, and export candidate stacks as a printable validation-swatch 3MF.

:::info[Forward color prediction]
Recipe Query calculates the resulting color from a filament stack you assemble. It does not perform an inverse search from a target HEX value.
:::

## Reading the stack order

Every stack is listed from **viewing face → substrate**:

- The first selected material sits on the outermost viewing surface.
- Each subsequent material is added closer to the substrate.
- Both the substrate (**White** or **Black**) and the color-layer height affect the predicted color.
- Reversing the same set of filaments changes the resulting color.

## Step 1: Choose substrate and layer height

Open **Material Management → Recipe Query**.

<img
  src={require('./assets/01-query-overview.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Material Recipe Query with substrate, layer height, recipe list, predicted color, queue, and material library areas highlighted"
/>

Configure the base conditions for your test:

- **White** or **Black** substrate to match your model's actual backing.
- The color-layer height matching your Material Archives and planned slicer profile (`0.04 mm`, `0.08 mm`, or `0.10 mm`).

Light transmission and coverage change with layer thickness, so keep the layer height consistent across all candidates queued onto the same swatch plate.

## Step 2: Find the target Material Archives

<img
  src={require('./assets/02-search-materials.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Recipe Query material search with sample Yellow and Blue archives highlighted"
/>

Filter the library by material name, brand, archive directory name, or HEX color. Before clicking a card, verify:

- Brand, product line, and physical filament match the spool you plan to load
- Archive directory and calibration source
- Layer-height compatibility

For guidance on choosing color sources, see [Choose Material Archives or a Classic LUT](../material-profile-selection/material-profile-selection.mdx).

## Step 3: Build the stack in order

Click material cards in sequence from the viewing face toward the substrate.

<img
  src={require('./assets/03-layer-order.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina recipe stack showing Yellow, Yellow, and Blue layers from viewing face to substrate"
/>

For example, selecting Yellow twice and then Blue builds:

```text
Viewing face → Yellow → Yellow → Blue → White substrate
```

Use the stack controls to adjust layers:

- **Undo last layer** removes the most recently added layer nearest the substrate.
- **Clear** removes all layers in the current stack.
- **Reverse** flips the entire layer sequence end to end.

Whenever you change the stack order, click **Compute color** to update the prediction.

## Step 4: Compare original and reversed stacks

<img
  src={require('./assets/04-order-changes-result.webp').default}
  alt="Real English comparison of Yellow Yellow Blue and Blue Yellow Yellow stacks with different predicted colors"
/>

Even with identical spools, changing the sequence alters how light is transmitted and absorbed:

| Stack from viewing face to substrate | Predicted color |
| --- | --- |
| Yellow → Yellow → Blue | `#99B268` |
| Blue → Yellow → Yellow | `#698E75` |

Always record the exact layer order alongside the filament names when saving a recipe.

:::note[Verify with a printed swatch]
Screen predictions help narrow down candidate stacks quickly, while real prints also depend on filament batch, moisture, nozzle temperature, flow, first-layer squish, and ambient lighting. Print a validation swatch and inspect it under neutral lighting before committing to a final piece.
:::

## Step 5: Queue candidates and configure swatch layout

Click **Add to queue** to save a computed stack, then modify or reverse the layers, compute again, and add other candidates you want to compare on the same plate.

<img
  src={require('./assets/05-queue-settings.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina Recipe queue with two candidate recipes and swatch size, spacing, columns, and single-sided settings highlighted"
/>

Configure the validation plate below the queue:

- **Swatch Size**: edge length of each square swatch (`5–50 mm`). Larger squares make surface texture and color uniformity easier to judge.
- **Spacing**: width of the white divider grid between squares (`0.5–10 mm`).
- **Columns**: set `0` for automatic grid layout, or specify a fixed column count (`1–20`).
- **Single-sided / Double-sided**: generate a single viewing face, or mirror the same stack on both sides of a central white backing.

## Step 6: Generate the 3MF and save the legend

Click **Generate swatch** and wait for **Download 3MF** and the numbered grid legend to appear.

<img
  src={require('./assets/06-swatch-ready.webp').default}
  alt="Real English Lumina generated validation swatch with Download 3MF and row-column legend highlighted"
/>

1. Click **Download 3MF** and save a screenshot of the row/column legend.
2. Open the 3MF **as a complete project** in your slicer.
3. Map every project filament to the corresponding physical spool and AMS or external slot.
4. Slice the plate and inspect the viewing face layer by layer.
5. Print with your target nozzle and color-layer height.
6. Match each square on the cooled print to the row/column legend under consistent, neutral lighting.

## Evaluating printed swatches

- Allow the plate to cool completely before judging gloss and color.
- Hold all candidates under the same neutral light source and viewing angle.
- Compare physical swatches directly rather than relying on phone photos with auto exposure and auto white balance.
- Check opacity, surface smoothness, and edge cleanliness alongside hue.
- Print a fresh swatch if you switch filament batches, layer heights, nozzles, or backing colors.

## Common questions

### Can I enter a target HEX color to find the closest stack automatically?

Recipe Query evaluates stacks you define manually. To match artwork colors to filament stacks automatically, use the standard Image Conversion workflow.

### Why does clicking Reverse change the predicted color?

Each filament layer filters light differently depending on whether it sits at the surface or beneath translucent layers. Because light transmission and coverage are directional, flipping the order produces a different visible color.

### Can I add the same filament more than once?

Yes. Consecutive layers of the same filament increase optical thickness at that position in the stack, altering how much underlying color shows through.

### How do I choose between White and Black substrate?

Match the backing color of your actual project. Note that the generated validation swatch uses a white grid and backing by default; if you are testing black-backed recipes, reassign the backing object to black filament in your slicer before printing.

### Can I copy the predicted HEX value directly into illustration software?

You can use it as an approximate screen swatch for planning, though monitor profiles and physical plastics render color differently. Keep the printed validation plate as your physical color reference.

### Can I put multiple recipes into a single 3MF?

Yes. Add each computed candidate to the queue, set the swatch dimensions and column layout, and click **Generate swatch** once to combine them onto a single plate.

### Why does the slicer display unexpected colors?

Verify that you opened the 3MF as a full project and mapped each project filament to the intended physical spool and hardware slot. See [Import a Lumina 3MF into the Slicer Correctly](pathname:///en/docs/tutorials/slicer-material-mapping/).
